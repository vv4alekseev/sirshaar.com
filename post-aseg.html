Survey-conditioned pre-training for label-efficient seismic fault detection

A short talk at the 2nd ASEG DISCOVER Symposium in Cairns on 28 October 2026, with Patrick Makuluni, Ryan T. Armstrong, Nasser Khalili and Stuart R. Clark. Session 3.5, Boardroom 2. The question: how far can the unlabelled target survey itself take a fault detector when expert labels are scarce?

Interpreters disagree

Fault labels are scarce, and the people who draw them disagree. The CRACKS benchmark labels the same Netherlands F3 sections by an expert and eight practitioners. Scored against the expert as line F1 on fault skeletons, the practitioners range from 0.16 to 0.47.

The usual answer is pre-training on synthetic data from ever more complex generators. The talk asks what the target survey can add, since it is always there and needs no labels.

Method

Three label-free routes pre-train one U-Net++ with a ResNet-50 encoder on the unlabelled F3 survey:

  • Self-supervised: mask real traces and reconstruct them.
  • Survey-conditioned synthetics: the survey's own traces with a simple fault generator, so the labels are exact and the realism comes from the data.
  • A physics-based generator calibrated to the survey's statistics and wells.

The network is then fine-tuned on a few labelled sections and scored exactly like the interpreters: line F1 against the expert, within a few pixels.

Results

Trained on the picks of the best practitioner alone, every model beats all eight practitioners. The best reaches line F1 0.58, where that practitioner scores 0.47. It recovers 64% of the expert's certain faults, against 47% for the practitioner, and 49% of the uncertain ones, against 34%.

With no CRACKS labels at all, the survey-calibrated generator scores 0.30 and beats five of the eight practitioners. Classic synthetics score 0.24.

Take-aways

  1. A model trained on one interpreter's picks beats that interpreter.
  2. With no labels, a survey-calibrated generator beats five of eight practitioners.
  3. Survey conditioning finds the ambiguous faults with few labels.
